Market week

Market review

The week to 2026-09-04 was a rotation story more than an index story. SPY returned just +0.1% on the week (source: ow_rotation, as of 2026-09-04), and the cross-asset frame stayed calm: VIX at 14.52, high-yield spreads (BAMLH0A0HYM2) tight at 2.65 as of 2026-09-03, the 10-year (DGS10) at 4.788 and 2s10s at 41.3bp. Beneath that flat tape, capital preference tilted hard toward hard assets and semiconductor supply, and away from software and cybersecurity.

Ag and commodities led everything

The standout of the week was the el-nino-ag-2026 basket (DBA, MOS, NTR, DE), up +6.6% and +6.5% in excess of SPY, and +12.9% over four weeks (source: ow_rotation). This is a real, dated dispersion, not a rounding effect against a flat benchmark. The plausible mechanism is a supply-tightening bid for ag inputs and grain, which the theme's declared evidence (NOAA ONI, CBOT corn/wheat front-month) is meant to test; I cannot confirm those operator-checked inputs are met from this week's data, so I treat the move as price leadership, not a validated fundamental thesis. It also rhymes with a broader commodity impulse: overnight, copper hit a record high and Brent neared $98 on Middle East shipping risk (ow_uw_headlines, 2026-09-08T00:48:29Z), with a reported strike on Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery lifting WTI (2026-09-08T00:23:19Z). Note that commodity headlines are current-session color, not part of the completed week; the counterevidence is that a supply-shock crude bid and an El Nino grain thesis are distinct drivers that happen to point the same way. The next observable is whether the basket holds its excess once crude's geopolitical premium fades.

Semis split by position in the stack

Within tech, leadership concentrated at the manufacturing end of the chain. Foundry (INTC, TSEM, TSM, UMC) rose +5.4% (+5.3% vs SPY, 4 of 4 members) and Memory/Storage (STX, WDC, MU, SNDK) +7.6% (+7.5%, 4 of 4) — the two strongest baskets in the frame. Computer/GPU (+3.4%) and Semi-Logic/ASIC (+1.8%) also outpaced. The contrast is with the software and design end: Semi-Cap/EDA fell -1.5%, Software/SaaS -4.7%, and Cybersecurity was the weakest basket at -6.9% (all as of 2026-09-04). At the ETF level XLK managed only +0.9% (+0.7% excess), so the semi strength is a within-sector dispersion, not a broad tech bid; XLY (-2.0%) and XLI (-1.1%) were the sector laggards. I will not read the software weakness as funding the semi strength — these are separate return streams. The read is that the market this week paid up for physical capacity and memory pricing over software multiples; the next tell is whether that survives ADBE's print (below) and any read-through to SaaS breadth.

Rates and policy: quiet, but a live hike debate

The macro frame barely moved on the week — DGS2 at 4.375, DGS10 at 4.788, both roughly unchanged, credit tight, VIX low. But the policy path is not dovish: the frame carries a 9/16 hike probability of 57.9 (as of 2026-09-07), and overnight flow shows BOJ hike bets strengthening the yen to 153.53 (ow_uw_headlines, 2026-09-08T00:34:24Z) and the 10-year JGB yield at 2.905% (2026-09-08T00:49:12Z). Low VIX and tight spreads are observations of current calm, not proof that a hike is priced or that complacency reigns; the 9/16 decision sits beyond the coming seven days and is later context, not next week's event.

One coverage limitation: this tenant's close-phase reports for the week carry no market-observation prose (only two candidate proposals dated 09-03 and 09-04), so the weekly narrative here rests on the rotation table, the session frame and current headlines rather than dated in-week commentary.

Outlook

Two threads carry into next week. First, the commodity/ag leadership. It can persist if the crude supply premium holds and the grain-supply thesis keeps working, but those are two engines and either can stall independently: a de-escalation in the Middle East would pull the geopolitical bid out of Brent and WTI without touching the ONI story, while a warmer ONI read would undercut ag without touching crude. The confirming observable is the el-nino-ag basket holding excess over SPY even on a day crude gives back its premium; the reverse risk is that the basket's +6.6% week was a crowded, single-week spike that mean-reverts. I hold no directional call on the basket — the operator-checked ONI and CBOT inputs are not dated in this week's evidence, so the thesis is unconfirmed either way.

Second, the semis-versus-software split. The question is whether memory and foundry leadership is a durable capacity/pricing regime or a one-week rotation. ADBE's report on 2026-09-10 is the nearest clean test of the software side: a strong print that reverses SaaS breadth would argue the week's software weakness was event-specific rather than a regime; a weak one would extend it. Watch Semi-Cap/EDA (-1.5% this week) — if design/tools rejoin the foundry-memory strength, the semi bid broadens; if it keeps lagging, the leadership stays narrowly at the physical end.

Third, rates. The 9/16 decision is later context, not next week, but the debate is live at 57.9 hike probability. Next week's confirming signal would be the front end (DGS2, now 4.375) drifting up alongside a firmer VIX; the calm frame — VIX 14.52, HY at 2.65 — is the thing that breaks first if the hike path repriced. I am not inferring what is priced from that calm; I am flagging it as the low-vol condition that a policy surprise would test. Reverse risk to the whole risk-on read is a genuine crude spike feeding an inflation-then-tightening loop, which the overnight Goldman Brent upgrade to $85 for Dec2026 (ow_uw_headlines, 2026-09-08T00:24:05Z) gestures at without confirming.
